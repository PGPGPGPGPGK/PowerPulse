PowerPulse is an experimental public beta operated by Pavanganesh Karnam in India.

1. Who operates PowerPulse

PowerPulse is operated by Pavanganesh Karnam, India. Privacy, access, correction, deletion, or other data-related requests can be sent to pavanganeshkarnam@gmail.com.

2. Who may use reporting features

Anyone may browse publicly available outage information. Community reporting features are intended only for people aged 18 or older. PowerPulse does not currently provide a parental-consent verification system and does not knowingly permit users under 18 to submit community outage reports.

3. Information PowerPulse processes

Anonymous account identifier. PowerPulse uses Firebase Anonymous Authentication so reports from the same anonymous account can be distinguished from reports submitted by other users. PowerPulse does not require a name, phone number, or email address for normal outage reporting.

Approximate report location. If you choose to use device location, your browser may obtain a more precise position. Before a community report is stored, PowerPulse deliberately coarsens the location to an approximately 100-metre geographic grid. The precise device coordinate is not stored in the report.

Device-location accuracy. Your browser may provide an accuracy estimate. PowerPulse may show this locally, but it is not stored in community report records.

Community outage observations and timestamps. PowerPulse stores whether you reported power out, still out, or power restored, together with the time the report was received.

Optional information. You may provide a street or road description and a community-reported possible outage reason. Do not enter names, phone numbers, house numbers, precise home addresses, or other unnecessary personal information.

Feedback. PowerPulse may provide an anonymous feedback form asking what happened or what you suggest, how it affected your experience, and why a change would help. It does not require your name, email, phone number, location, account identifier, or other identifying details.

4. Precise device location

  • Your browser obtains device location only when you choose to use the location feature.
  • The more precise device position stays in the browser for local functionality.
  • Before a report is sent for storage, PowerPulse converts the position to a coarser approximately 100-metre location.
  • Only the coarsened report position is stored.
  • Other users are shown incident-level approximate locations and report clusters, not individual household locations.

5. Why PowerPulse uses this information

  • Group geographically nearby reports into community incidents.
  • Distinguish independent reporters from repeated observations by the same anonymous user.
  • Show whether an outage appears current and when it was last confirmed.
  • Process still-out and restoration confirmations.
  • Show approximate nearby outage activity.
  • Detect, investigate, and reduce misuse, spam, or manipulation.
  • Operate, debug, evaluate, and improve the experimental public beta.

PowerPulse does not use community report data for advertising or behavioural profiling.

6. What other users can see

PowerPulse does not publicly display Firebase anonymous user identifiers or individual reporter positions. It may display aggregated information such as reporter counts, approximate incident locations and radii, latest confirmation times, optional street descriptions, and community-reported possible reasons.

An incident circle means reports have been clustered around that area. It does not mean every property inside the circle is without power.

7. Third-party services

Firebase (Google). Used for anonymous authentication and Cloud Firestore data storage. Firebase Authentication may process technical information such as IP addresses and user-agent strings for authentication, security, and abuse prevention.

GitHub Pages. Used to host the public PowerPulse website.

OpenFreeMap / OpenStreetMap-derived map data. The interactive map loads map resources from OpenFreeMap and its CDN. This creates third-party network requests. PowerPulse does not send community report records or reporter identities to the map provider.

8. Data retention

PowerPulse's intended operational retention period for individual community report records is up to 30 days, unless a longer period is reasonably required for security, abuse investigation, debugging, legal obligations, or protection of the service.

Because PowerPulse is still an experimental public beta and automated deletion is being implemented, some existing records may temporarily remain for longer than 30 days. This policy will be updated when automated retention controls are active.

9. Your choices and rights

You may choose not to use device location and instead use a manual approximate location. Subject to applicable law, you may contact PowerPulse to ask about personal-data processing or request access, correction, or deletion of information associated with you.

Because normal reporting uses an anonymous account and does not require your name or email address, PowerPulse may not always be able to identify which anonymous records belong to you after your browser identity is cleared, lost, or changed.

10. Browser storage

PowerPulse and Firebase may use browser storage required to maintain an anonymous authentication session, remember application state, and remember local legal or age acknowledgement. Clearing browser/site data may reset your anonymous session.

11. Security

PowerPulse uses Firebase Authentication, Firestore Security Rules, append-only community report records, restricted client writes, and geographic coarsening of report locations. No internet service can guarantee absolute security.

12. Changes to this Privacy Policy

PowerPulse may update this Privacy Policy as the beta develops. Material changes will be published on this page with an updated effective date.
